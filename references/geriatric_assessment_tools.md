# Geriatric Assessment Tools

Consult this for any geriatric-syndrome case (falls, functional decline, cognitive concerns, incontinence) or whenever the user performs a cognitive or functional assessment on an older patient in any case. As the patient, respond realistically and consistently with the case's underlying picture (e.g. a patient with early cognitive impairment should miss specific, plausible items, not fail everything and not pass everything).

## Functional status

**ADLs (basic activities of daily living):** bathing, dressing, toileting, transferring (bed/chair), continence, feeding.

**IADLs (instrumental activities of daily living):** managing finances, managing medications, shopping, preparing meals, housekeeping and laundry, transportation or driving, using the telephone.

A good candidate screens both when functional decline is a concern. IADL loss usually comes before ADL loss, so IADL problems are often the earliest sign of trouble.

## Cognitive screening

Do not recite or reproduce the items of any copyrighted instrument. If the user names a tool and administers it, respond item by item to what they actually ask, and keep performance consistent with the case.

- **Mini-Cog** (by S. Borson; free for clinical and educational use): three-word registration, a clock-drawing task, then three-word recall. Scoring combines words recalled with whether the clock is normal. A low recall score, or partial recall with an abnormal clock, screens positive. A positive screen means further cognitive evaluation is warranted; it is not a diagnosis. Usually the most realistic choice in a short station.
- **Longer instruments** (e.g. MMSE, MoCA, both copyrighted): these cover broad domains such as orientation, registration and recall, attention and calculation, language, and visuospatial function. If the user administers one, answer each task they give in a way that fits the case (e.g. mild impairment means missing a point or two on recall or attention, not across every domain). Don't supply the wording of the items yourself.

## Grading notes

- Give credit for choosing a tool that fits the station's time limit.
- Give credit for screening ADLs and IADLs even without a formal cognitive test; functional screening alone is a reasonable and common approach.
- If the case doesn't specify a cognitive deficit, the patient performs normally on whichever tool is used.
