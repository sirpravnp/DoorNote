# Case: Low Back Pain (Ambulatory)

**Setting:** Primary care / outpatient IM clinic
**Suggested time:** 10-12 minutes total

---
## FOR CLAUDE ONLY -- do not reveal this section directly; deliver only through the encounter flow
---

### Patient demographics
Name: Denise Ferraro. Age 44. Female. Works as a warehouse packer (frequent lifting/bending). Affect: frustrated -- this has been dragging on and is affecting her ability to work.

### Primary diagnosis
Mechanical (musculoskeletal) low back pain, likely lumbar strain, related to repetitive lifting at work.

### Key differentials
- **Cauda equina syndrome [RED FLAG]** -- must be actively screened for and ruled out by history/exam.
- **Spinal malignancy / metastatic disease [RED FLAG]** -- low pretest probability here (no cancer history, no red flags on screening) but must be screened for.
- **Vertebral infection/epidural abscess [RED FLAG]** -- screen for fever, IVDU, recent infection.
- Lumbar disc herniation with radiculopathy (mild -- some radicular features present but no motor/sensory deficit or red flags)

### History key (reveal only as asked)
- **Onset:** 5 weeks ago, after a shift where she lifted several heavy boxes repeatedly. Gradual worsening, not one sudden "pop."
- **Location:** Bilateral lower lumbar region, slightly worse on the right.
- **Character:** Dull, aching, sometimes sharp with certain movements (bending forward, twisting).
- **Radiation:** Occasionally radiates into the right buttock and upper thigh, does NOT go below the knee, no radiation into the foot.
- **Aggravating:** Bending, lifting, prolonged standing at work.
- **Relieving:** Rest, ibuprofen (partial relief), lying flat.
- **Severity:** 6/10 at worst, 3/10 at baseline.
- **Timing:** Constant low-level ache with intermittent flares; NOT worse at night specifically, NOT waking her from sleep (important negative against malignancy/infection).
- **Red flag screening (if asked):**
  - **No bowel or bladder incontinence or retention.**
  - **No saddle anesthesia (numbness in groin/inner thighs/perineum).**
  - No bilateral leg weakness or numbness.
  - No unexplained weight loss.
  - No fever or chills.
  - No history of cancer.
  - No IV drug use.
  - No recent infection (dental, skin, urinary) that could seed an epidural abscess.
  - No trauma beyond the repetitive lifting (no fall, no motor vehicle accident).
  - No history of osteoporosis or steroid use (fracture risk).
- **PMH:** Otherwise healthy. No prior back problems of this severity, though occasional mild aches in the past.
- **Medications:** OTC ibuprofen as needed, no prescription meds.
- **Allergies:** NKDA.
- **Family history:** Noncontributory (no cancer, no ankylosing spondylitis or inflammatory disease).
- **Social history:** No smoking, occasional alcohol, no drug use. Physically demanding job (repetitive lifting, some hours on her feet). Worried about losing income if she can't work.
- **ROS:** Denies fevers, denies unintentional weight loss, denies morning stiffness lasting more than 30 minutes (argues against inflammatory/ankylosing spondylitis pattern), denies joint swelling elsewhere.

### Vitals (give on request)
BP 124/78, HR 74, RR 14, Temp 98.2°F, O2 sat 99% RA, BMI 26.

### Physical exam findings (reveal only for specific system/maneuver requested)
- **General:** Uncomfortable but non-toxic appearing, moves gingerly getting on/off exam table.
- **Inspection of spine:** No visible deformity, no step-off, no skin changes/rash over the spine.
- **Palpation:** Tenderness over the paraspinal muscles bilaterally (right > left), no point tenderness over the spinous processes themselves (argues against fracture).
- **Range of motion:** Reduced lumbar flexion due to pain, otherwise normal extension/lateral bending.
- **Straight leg raise (right):** Mild reproduction of back/buttock discomfort at ~70 degrees, does NOT reproduce radicular pain below the knee (negative straight leg raise for true radiculopathy).
- **Straight leg raise (left):** Negative.
- **Motor exam (lower extremities):** 5/5 strength throughout, including hip flexion, knee extension/flexion, ankle dorsi/plantarflexion, great toe extension.
- **Sensory exam (lower extremities):** Intact to light touch throughout, including perianal/saddle region if specifically tested.
- **Reflexes:** 2+ patellar and Achilles bilaterally, symmetric.
- **Rectal tone (if specifically requested -- appropriate to mention verbally rather than perform in this format):** Normal tone, no evidence of cauda equina on this exam.
- **Gait:** Antalgic but able to walk, no foot drop, able to heel/toe walk.

### Labs/imaging (give only if ordered)
- **Plain lumbar X-ray (if ordered):** Not clinically indicated this early without red flags; if ordered anyway -- mild degenerative changes, no fracture, no lytic lesions.
- **MRI lumbar spine (if ordered):** Not indicated without red flags or persistent radiculopathy at this stage; if ordered anyway -- mild disc bulge at L4-5, no significant canal stenosis, no cord/cauda equina compression.
- **ESR/CRP (if ordered):** Within normal limits (helps argue against infection/inflammatory cause).
- **CBC (if ordered):** Unremarkable.

### Model answer for debrief
This is most consistent with mechanical low back pain / lumbar strain from repetitive occupational lifting, supported by a gradual onset tied to a specific activity, pain localized to the paraspinal region without true radicular features below the knee, a negative straight leg raise for radicular pain, and a fully intact neurologic exam. Cauda equina syndrome, spinal malignancy, and infection were appropriately screened for and are not supported here -- no bowel/bladder dysfunction, no saddle anesthesia, no fever, no weight loss, no cancer history. Per current guidelines, imaging is not indicated at this stage without red flags. Plan: NSAIDs, activity modification, encourage staying active rather than strict bed rest, consider physical therapy, address ergonomics/workplace modifications, and give explicit red-flag return precautions so cauda equina or another serious cause wouldn't be missed if it developed.
