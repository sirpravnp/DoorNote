# Case: Falls and Functional Decline (Geriatric Syndrome, Ambulatory)

**Setting:** Primary care / outpatient IM clinic
**Suggested time:** 10-12 minutes total

---
## FOR CLAUDE ONLY -- do not reveal this section directly; deliver only through the encounter flow

### Patient demographics
Name: Eleanor Vasquez. Age 79. Female. Widowed, lives alone in a two-story house. Brought in by her daughter, who is worried. Affect: a little defensive about her independence, downplays the falls at first ("I just tripped, it's nothing").

### Primary diagnosis
Multifactorial fall risk: polypharmacy (including a benzodiazepine and an anticholinergic), mild orthostatic hypotension, and early functional decline with mild cognitive impairment (screens positive on Mini-Cog but not full dementia).

### Key differentials
- Polypharmacy/medication-related fall risk (part of the actual picture)
- Orthostatic hypotension (part of the actual picture)
- **Cardiac arrhythmia/syncope [RED FLAG]** -- should be screened for via history even though this case's falls are mechanical/multifactorial, not syncopal.
- Early neurocognitive disorder (mild, screened via Mini-Cog -- relevant contributor, not the sole cause)
- Musculoskeletal/gait-related (mild lower extremity weakness, deconditioning)

### History key (reveal only as asked)
- **Falls history:** Two falls in the past 3 months. First one: tripped on a rug at home, no loss of consciousness, minor bruising. Second one: about 3 weeks ago, got up quickly from the couch and "felt dizzy" before going down, no injury that time either. No falls before this in her life that she recalls.
- **Associated symptoms with falls:** Denies chest pain, denies palpitations, denies loss of consciousness with either fall (helps argue against a cardiac/syncopal cause), does describe brief lightheadedness with the second fall, especially on standing quickly.
- **Gait/balance:** Admits she's felt "less steady" over the past several months, holds onto furniture when walking around the house now, hasn't mentioned this to anyone before.
- **Vision/hearing:** Vision "not what it used to be" but wears glasses, hasn't had an eye exam in 3 years. Hearing seems fine.
- **Home safety:** Lives alone, two-story house, does have some loose rugs, bathroom without grab bars, no stairlift.
- **Functional status (ADLs/IADLs -- reveal if asked):**
  - ADLs: independent in bathing, dressing, toileting, eating, transfers, grooming -- all intact.
  - IADLs: daughter has started helping with medication management over the last couple months because Eleanor "sometimes forgets if she took her pills." Still drives short distances but daughter is starting to worry about this. Manages her own finances but has had a couple of late bill payments recently. Cooking and light housework still independent.
- **Cognitive concerns (reveal if asked):** Some mild forgetfulness noticed by her daughter over the past 6 months -- repeating questions occasionally, misplacing items more than before. No getting lost in familiar places. No major personality change. No difficulty recognizing family.
- **Medications (important -- reveal in full if medication list is requested):** Lorazepam 0.5mg at bedtime (started about a year ago for sleep, taken nightly), diphenhydramine occasionally for allergies/sleep, amlodipine for blood pressure, a statin, and a multivitamin.
- **PMH:** Hypertension, mild osteoarthritis in both knees, insomnia (why she's on the lorazepam).
- **Allergies:** NKDA.
- **Family history:** Noncontributory for early dementia; mother lived independently into her 90s.
- **Social history:** Lives alone since her husband passed 2 years ago. No alcohol. Non-smoker. Daughter lives 20 minutes away and checks in a few times a week.
- **Red flag screening (if asked):**
  - No chest pain or palpitations with falls.
  - No loss of consciousness.
  - No fevers, no new infection symptoms (would be relevant in an elderly patient with new falls).
  - No new focal neurologic symptoms (weakness, numbness, slurred speech).

### Vitals (give on request -- orthostatics are a key finding here, reveal as a set if orthostatic vitals specifically requested)
- **Supine:** BP 138/82, HR 74.
- **Standing (after 1-3 minutes):** BP 112/68, HR 88 -- a drop of 26 systolic, meeting criteria for orthostatic hypotension, with associated symptoms (lightheadedness) reproduced on standing.
- Temp 98.2°F, RR 14, O2 sat 97% RA.

### Physical exam findings (reveal only for specific system/maneuver requested)
- **General:** Well-groomed, cooperative, mildly anxious about being "in trouble."
- **Cardiac auscultation:** Regular rate and rhythm, no murmurs.
- **Lung auscultation:** Clear bilaterally.
- **Neuro exam:** Grossly intact strength and sensation throughout, no focal deficits.
- **Gait assessment (Timed Up and Go, if performed):** Takes over 12 seconds (prolonged; normal is under ~10-12 seconds), noticeably cautious and slow, uses the armrest to push up from the chair.
- **Vision screen (if performed):** Mildly reduced visual acuity, consistent with needing an updated glasses prescription.

### Cognitive/functional screening (reveal only if the user administers it -- see references/geriatric_assessment_tools.md for screening guidance)
- **Mini-Cog:** Recalls 1 of 3 words after the clock-drawing task; clock draw is mildly abnormal (numbers slightly crowded on one side, but hands roughly correct). This combination screens **positive**, suggesting further cognitive workup is warranted -- consistent with mild impairment, not a clear dementia picture.
- **ADL screen:** All intact (see history above).
- **IADL screen:** Medication management and driving are the two areas of emerging concern (see history above).

### Labs/imaging (give only if ordered)
- **CBC, BMP:** Unremarkable, no anemia, no electrolyte abnormality.
- **ECG (if ordered, given falls workup):** Normal sinus rhythm, no arrhythmia.
- **B12/folate (if ordered):** Normal.
- **TSH (if ordered):** Normal.

### Model answer for debrief
This is a multifactorial fall risk picture: a fall-risk-increasing medication regimen (a nightly benzodiazepine plus an as-needed anticholinergic antihistamine), objective orthostatic hypotension reproducing her lightheadedness on standing, and early functional/cognitive changes (a positive Mini-Cog screen and emerging IADL difficulty with medications and driving) layered on top of home safety hazards. Cardiac syncope is an important differential to actively screen for in any elderly patient with falls but is well argued against here by the absence of loss of consciousness, chest pain, or palpitations, and a normal cardiac exam. Plan: address the medication list first (taper the benzodiazepine, stop the anticholinergic), manage orthostatic hypotension, arrange a home safety evaluation and consider PT for gait/balance, get her vision reassessed, and pursue further cognitive evaluation given the positive screen -- which also means having a frank, sensitive conversation about driving safety with her and her daughter.
