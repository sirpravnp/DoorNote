# Case: Osteoporosis Risk Assessment (Ambulatory)

**Setting:** Primary care / outpatient IM clinic -- routine visit, DEXA results review
**Suggested time:** 10-12 minutes total

---
## FOR CLAUDE ONLY -- do not reveal this section directly; deliver only through the encounter flow
---

### Patient demographics
Name: Barbara Sung. Age 67. Female. Postmenopausal (menopause at 51). Retired schoolteacher. Affect: a little anxious after "Googling" her DEXA result before the visit.

### Primary diagnosis
Osteoporosis (confirmed by DEXA T-score), with several modifiable and non-modifiable risk factors, no fracture yet.

### Key differentials
- Osteoporosis (the diagnosis, per DEXA criteria)
- Osteopenia (ruled out here since her T-score meets osteoporosis threshold, but worth knowing the distinction)
- **Secondary osteoporosis [screen, not the answer here]** -- causes like hyperthyroidism, hyperparathyroidism, chronic steroid use, or malabsorption should be screened for by history even though this case is primary/age-related osteoporosis.

### History key (reveal only as asked)
- **How this came up:** Routine screening DEXA ordered at her last physical, given her age; results came back showing osteoporosis at the hip and spine.
- **Fracture history:** No prior fragility fractures. No falls in the past year. Denies any recent height loss she's aware of, though admits she hasn't been measured in a while.
- **Menopause history:** Natural menopause at age 51, no hormone replacement therapy used.
- **Risk factor screening (if asked):**
  - **Family history:** Her mother had a hip fracture in her late 70s.
  - **Body frame:** Describes herself as "always been thin," current weight is on the lower end of normal BMI.
  - **Smoking:** Former smoker, quit 15 years ago, about a 10 pack-year history total.
  - **Alcohol:** Social drinker, doesn't exceed recommended limits.
  - **Steroid use:** No chronic steroid use; had a short prednisone taper for a bad poison ivy reaction once, years ago, not chronic.
  - **Calcium/Vitamin D intake:** Doesn't take a supplement, diet is "not great" for dairy/calcium-rich foods, limited sun exposure since she avoids being outside during peak hours.
  - **Physical activity:** Mostly sedentary, walks occasionally but no regular weight-bearing or resistance exercise.
  - **Secondary cause screen:** No symptoms of hyperthyroidism (no heat intolerance, no palpitations, no significant weight loss). No symptoms suggesting hyperparathyroidism (no kidney stones, no significant constipation, no bone pain). No malabsorption symptoms (no chronic diarrhea, no significant weight loss).
- **PMH:** Mild hypothyroidism, well-controlled on levothyroxine (worth confirming she isn't over-replaced, since over-treatment can itself contribute to bone loss).
- **Medications:** Levothyroxine, a multivitamin (without calcium/vitamin D specifically), occasional ibuprofen for joint aches.
- **Allergies:** NKDA.
- **Social history:** Lives with her husband, home has a couple of stairs but no other major fall hazards that she's aware of.
- **ROS:** Denies back pain, denies height loss, denies recent fractures, denies GI symptoms.

### Vitals (give on request)
BP 126/78, HR 70, RR 14, Temp 98.2°F, O2 sat 98% RA, BMI 20.5 (low-normal, itself a risk factor), height measured today vs. her stated height from years ago (if compared, shows about a 1cm reduction -- mild, not dramatically concerning, but worth tracking).

### Physical exam findings (reveal only for specific system/maneuver requested)
- **General:** Thin build, no acute distress.
- **Spine inspection/palpation:** No significant kyphosis, no point tenderness over the vertebrae (no signs of an occult vertebral compression fracture).
- **Height/weight:** As above.
- **Gait and balance (if assessed):** Normal, steady gait, no fall risk features on brief observation.
- **Thyroid palpation:** Normal size, no nodules, non-tender (relevant given her hypothyroidism history and to screen for any new thyroid pathology).

### Labs/imaging (give only if ordered, or reference DEXA results that prompted the visit if asked)
- **DEXA scan (the result she's here to discuss):** T-score -2.7 at the femoral neck, -2.5 at the lumbar spine -- both meet criteria for osteoporosis (T-score ≤ -2.5).
- **Calcium (if ordered):** Normal.
- **Vitamin D (25-OH, if ordered):** Low-normal, insufficient (a common and correctable contributor).
- **TSH (if ordered):** Within normal range, confirming her levothyroxine dose is appropriate and not a contributing secondary cause.
- **PTH (if ordered, secondary cause screen):** Normal.
- **FRAX score (if the user calculates or discusses one):** Elevated 10-year fracture risk consistent with treatment being indicated, given her T-score and risk factors (age, family history of hip fracture, low BMI, former smoking).

### Model answer for debrief
Her DEXA results meet criteria for osteoporosis (T-score ≤ -2.5 at both the hip and spine), in the setting of several real risk factors: postmenopausal status without hormone therapy, low BMI, a family history of hip fracture, a former smoking history, and low vitamin D intake. Secondary causes are worth actively screening for, particularly making sure her thyroid replacement isn't over-dosed (over-treated hypothyroidism itself accelerates bone loss) and ruling out hyperparathyroidism, but nothing here points to a secondary cause -- this is primary, age/menopause-related osteoporosis. Plan: calcium and vitamin D supplementation, weight-bearing exercise, pharmacotherapy (typically a bisphosphonate as first-line) given her T-score and elevated fracture risk, fall-prevention counseling, and a follow-up DEXA in 1-2 years to assess treatment response. Addressing her anxiety about the diagnosis with clear, factual information is an important part of a strong answer here.
