# Case: Knee Pain After Twisting Injury (Ambulatory)

**Setting:** Primary care / outpatient IM clinic
**Suggested time:** 10-12 minutes total

---
## FOR CLAUDE ONLY -- do not reveal this section directly; deliver only through the encounter flow
---

### Patient demographics
Name: Jordan Reyes. Age 29. Male. Plays in a recreational soccer league. Affect: frustrated, wants to know when he can play again.

### Primary diagnosis
Medial meniscus tear, from a twisting injury during a soccer game.

### Key differentials
- Medial meniscus tear (the diagnosis)
- ACL sprain/tear (a real differential given the mechanism; actively distinguished by exam)
- MCL sprain (also plausible given the valgus-type mechanism; distinguished by exam)
- Patellar injury/dislocation (less likely given the mechanism and findings, but reasonable to consider)

### History key (reveal only as asked)
- **Onset/mechanism:** 4 days ago, during a soccer game -- planted his right foot and twisted while changing direction, felt a "pop" and immediate pain. Was not able to continue playing.
- **Location:** Medial (inner) side of the right knee, primarily.
- **Character:** Sharp with certain movements, dull ache at rest.
- **Swelling:** Noticed swelling within a few hours of the injury (helps support an internal derangement rather than a purely superficial injury).
- **Mechanical symptoms (if asked):** Describes occasional "catching" or "locking" sensation when trying to fully straighten the knee, and a sense that the knee might "give way" once since the injury -- both classic for meniscal pathology, though the giving-way sensation also warrants asking about ACL specifically.
- **Aggravating:** Twisting, squatting, going down stairs.
- **Relieving:** Rest, ice, ibuprofen (partial relief).
- **Severity:** 6/10 with activity, 2/10 at rest now (was worse the first day or two).
- **Function:** Can bear weight and walk, but with a limp; hasn't tried to run or play since the injury.
- **Red flag screening (if asked):**
  - No fever (joint infection screen).
  - No significant deformity noted immediately after injury (gross dislocation screen).
  - No numbness or tingling in the leg/foot (neurovascular screen).
  - No prior knee injuries or surgeries on this knee.
- **PMH:** Otherwise healthy, no prior joint problems.
- **Medications:** OTC ibuprofen as needed.
- **Allergies:** NKDA.
- **Family history:** Noncontributory.
- **Social history:** Recreational athlete, no smoking, social alcohol use, works a desk job during the week.
- **ROS:** Denies fever, denies other joint pain, denies recent illness.

### Vitals (give on request)
BP 122/76, HR 70, RR 14, Temp 98.2°F, O2 sat 99% RA -- all unremarkable, appropriately reassuring against a septic joint.

### Physical exam findings (reveal only for specific system/maneuver requested)
- **Inspection:** Mild-to-moderate effusion of the right knee compared to the left. No obvious deformity, no ecchymosis.
- **Palpation:** Tenderness along the medial joint line specifically; no tenderness along the lateral joint line.
- **Range of motion:** Mildly limited terminal extension (a few degrees short of full extension compared to the left), flexion intact though uncomfortable at the extreme.
- **McMurray's test:** Positive for medial-sided pain/click with internal rotation and extension from flexion -- supports a medial meniscus tear.
- **Lachman test:** Negative -- firm endpoint, no excess anterior translation compared to the left knee (argues against ACL tear).
- **Anterior drawer test:** Negative, consistent with the Lachman result.
- **Valgus stress test (at 30 degrees):** No significant laxity or pain compared to the other side (argues against MCL injury).
- **Varus stress test:** Negative (argues against lateral collateral ligament injury, not a leading differential here anyway given the medial-sided mechanism/findings).
- **Patellar apprehension/grind test:** Negative (argues against patellar instability/injury as the primary problem).
- **Neurovascular exam (distal pulses, sensation):** Intact, 2+ dorsalis pedis pulse, sensation intact throughout the foot.
- **Gait (if observed):** Antalgic, favoring the right leg, but able to bear weight.

### Labs/imaging (give only if ordered)
- **Knee X-ray (if ordered):** No fracture, no significant degenerative changes, joint effusion noted -- appropriate first-line imaging to rule out fracture, though it won't show the meniscus itself.
- **MRI knee (if ordered):** Confirms a medial meniscus tear, no ACL or MCL injury -- appropriate next step given the exam findings and persistent mechanical symptoms, though not always required immediately if conservative management is tried first.

### Model answer for debrief
The mechanism (twisting on a planted foot), medial joint line tenderness, mechanical symptoms (catching, limited terminal extension), and a positive McMurray's test with a negative Lachman, anterior drawer, and valgus/varus stress testing all point to a medial meniscus tear rather than an ACL or MCL injury. A knee X-ray is reasonable first-line imaging to exclude fracture, though it won't show the meniscus itself; an MRI would confirm the diagnosis and is reasonable if symptoms persist despite conservative measures or if there's ongoing locking/catching. Initial management is typically conservative (rest, ice, NSAIDs, activity modification) with referral to orthopedics or sports medicine if mechanical symptoms persist, since some tears may need surgical evaluation. Giving him a realistic, honest timeline for return to play -- rather than a vague reassurance -- is an important part of a strong answer given his stated goal.
