# Case: Elevated Blood Pressure (Hypertension, Ambulatory)

**Setting:** Primary care / outpatient IM clinic -- follow-up after elevated readings at a previous visit and pharmacy screening
**Suggested time:** 10-12 minutes total

---
## FOR CLAUDE ONLY -- do not reveal this section directly; deliver only through the encounter flow
---

### Patient demographics
Name: Patricia Nwosu. Age 54. Female. Works as an office manager. Affect: mildly anxious about "being put on medication for the rest of my life."

### Primary diagnosis
Newly confirmed essential (primary) hypertension, stage 2, without evidence of secondary cause or end-organ damage on initial workup.

### Key differentials
- Essential (primary) hypertension (the diagnosis)
- **Secondary hypertension [screen, not the answer here]** -- renal, endocrine (e.g. primary aldosteronism, pheochromocytoma, thyroid), or obstructive sleep apnea-related; should be screened for by history given her age and severity, but nothing in this case points toward a secondary cause.
- White-coat hypertension -- reasonably excluded here by a home BP log showing consistently elevated readings, not just in-office readings.

### History key (reveal only as asked)
- **How this came up:** Elevated BP found at a pharmacy blood pressure check 6 weeks ago (168/98), confirmed elevated again at an office visit 3 weeks ago (162/96). She's been keeping a home BP log since then as instructed.
- **Home readings (reveal if specifically asked for her log):** Averaging around 150-160 systolic and 90-96 diastolic over the past 3 weeks, fairly consistent, checked both morning and evening on most days -- confirms sustained hypertension rather than an isolated or white-coat reading.
- **Symptoms:** Mostly asymptomatic, which is typical -- if asked, denies headaches, denies chest pain, denies vision changes, denies shortness of breath. Occasionally notices mild headaches on stressful workdays but attributes it to stress, not consistently tied to BP readings.
- **Red flag/secondary cause screening (if asked):**
  - No episodic symptoms suggestive of pheochromocytoma (episodic headache, palpitations, sweating, pallor).
  - No muscle weakness or cramping (would raise concern for hyperaldosteronism/hypokalemia).
  - No snoring or witnessed apneas reported (OSA screen -- though her husband isn't here to confirm; worth asking her directly about daytime fatigue too, which she denies).
  - No hematuria, no significant flank pain, no known kidney disease (renal cause screen).
  - No recent NSAID use, no decongestant/stimulant use, no oral contraceptive use, no licorice ingestion (medication/substance-related secondary causes).
- **PMH:** No other major medical history. No prior diagnosis of hypertension before this.
- **Medications:** Occasional ibuprofen for headaches (relevant -- NSAIDs can raise BP, worth asking about frequency), otherwise no regular medications.
- **Allergies:** NKDA.
- **Family history:** Mother and older sister both have hypertension; father had a stroke in his 60s.
- **Social history:** Sedentary desk job, minimal regular exercise. Diet is high in sodium (admits to a lot of packaged/processed food, eats out frequently). Drinks 1-2 glasses of wine most nights. No smoking. High-stress job. Sleep is about 6 hours/night, doesn't feel particularly rested but attributes this to being busy, not to any specific sleep disorder symptom.
- **ROS:** Denies chest pain, denies palpitations, denies vision changes, denies shortness of breath, denies leg swelling.

### Vitals (give on request)
Repeat in-office BP (average of 2 readings): 158/94. HR 76. RR 14. Temp 98.2°F. O2 sat 98% RA. BMI 29.

### Physical exam findings (reveal only for specific system/maneuver requested)
- **General:** Well-appearing, no distress.
- **Cardiac auscultation:** Regular rate and rhythm, no murmurs, no S3/S4 gallop (relevant negative for early heart failure from long standing hypertension).
- **Lung auscultation:** Clear bilaterally, no crackles (relevant negative for pulmonary edema/heart failure).
- **Abdominal exam:** Soft, non-tender, no abdominal bruit (relevant negative -- an abdominal bruit would raise concern for renal artery stenosis as a secondary cause).
- **Lower extremities:** No edema.
- **Fundoscopic exam (if performed):** No hypertensive retinopathy changes noted (no AV nicking, no hemorrhages, no papilledema).
- **Thyroid palpation:** Normal size, no nodules (relevant to screening thyroid as a secondary cause).

### Labs/imaging (give only if ordered)
- **BMP (electrolytes, creatinine):** Normal, including normal potassium (a low potassium would raise suspicion for hyperaldosteronism).
- **Urinalysis:** No proteinuria, no hematuria.
- **Lipid panel:** Mildly elevated LDL, otherwise unremarkable -- worth addressing as part of overall cardiovascular risk.
- **TSH:** Normal.
- **ECG (if ordered):** Normal sinus rhythm, no evidence of left ventricular hypertrophy.
- **Fasting glucose/A1c (if ordered):** Normal, not diabetic.

### Model answer for debrief
This is newly confirmed stage 2 essential hypertension, supported by consistently elevated home readings over 3 weeks and a confirmatory elevated in-office reading, with a reassuring exam (no bruit, no retinopathy, no gallop) and normal baseline labs arguing against a secondary cause or existing end-organ damage. Secondary causes -- renal, endocrine, and OSA -- are appropriately screened for by history and are not supported here. White-coat hypertension is excluded by the home log showing sustained elevation outside the office. Given the severity (stage 2) and her cardiovascular risk factors (family history, mildly elevated LDL, sedentary lifestyle), starting pharmacotherapy now alongside lifestyle counseling is appropriate rather than trialing lifestyle changes alone first. Baseline labs and an ECG are reasonable to obtain both to screen for secondary causes/end-organ damage and to establish a baseline before starting treatment. Addressing her anxiety about long-term medication directly, rather than only reciting the plan, is an important part of a strong answer.
