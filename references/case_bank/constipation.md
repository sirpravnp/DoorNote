# Case: Chronic Constipation (Ambulatory)

**Setting:** Primary care / outpatient IM clinic
**Suggested time:** 10-12 minutes total

---
## FOR CLAUDE ONLY -- do not reveal this section directly; deliver only through the encounter flow
---

### Patient demographics
Name: Harold Bhatt. Age 58. Male. Works as an accountant (sedentary job). Affect: a little embarrassed discussing bowel habits, tends to give brief answers unless asked directly.

### Primary diagnosis
Functional (primary) constipation, related to low fiber intake, sedentary lifestyle, and inadequate hydration -- no red-flag features present, and he's not yet had age-appropriate colorectal cancer screening, which is a real opportunity in this visit.

### Key differentials
- Functional constipation (the diagnosis)
- Medication-induced constipation (worth screening for -- he's not on a major culprit medication in this case, but it's an important category to rule out)
- Hypothyroidism-related constipation (worth screening for as a secondary cause)
- **Colorectal malignancy [RED FLAG]** -- low likelihood here but must be actively screened for given his age and the fact that he's overdue for colorectal cancer screening.

### History key (reveal only as asked)
- **Onset/duration:** Gradual over the past several months, would say at least 6 months of infrequent, hard stools.
- **Frequency:** Down to about 2 bowel movements per week, used to be closer to daily.
- **Character:** Hard, lumpy stools (consistent with a low-fiber, slow-transit pattern), significant straining, sensation of incomplete evacuation.
- **Associated symptoms:** Mild bloating and abdominal discomfort that improves after a bowel movement. No significant abdominal pain otherwise.
- **Red flag screening (if asked):**
  - **No blood in the stool or on wiping** (though he hasn't looked closely -- if pressed further, he says he "hasn't noticed" any, which should be taken as a true negative for this case).
  - **No unintentional weight loss.**
  - No fevers.
  - No family history of colorectal cancer or inflammatory bowel disease that he's aware of.
  - No nausea or vomiting.
  - No change in stool caliber (e.g. pencil-thin stools) that he's noticed.
- **Diet/lifestyle:** Diet is low in fiber -- mostly processed foods, minimal fruits/vegetables/whole grains. Drinks maybe 2-3 glasses of water a day, several cups of coffee. Very sedentary -- desk job, minimal exercise.
- **Bowel habits/behavior:** Admits he often ignores the urge to go when busy at work and "holds it" until he gets home, sometimes for hours.
- **Screening history:** Has never had a colonoscopy -- when asked why, says his doctor mentioned it a while back but he never scheduled it. This is a real gap given he's 58 (colorectal cancer screening is recommended starting at 45).
- **Thyroid screen (if asked):** No cold intolerance, no significant weight change, no fatigue beyond what he attributes to being busy -- reassuring against hypothyroidism as a driver, though still reasonable to check.
- **PMH:** Mild hypertension, otherwise healthy.
- **Medications:** Amlodipine for blood pressure (not a major constipating agent, unlike some other antihypertensives or opioids) -- worth explicitly confirming he isn't on an opioid, iron supplement, or anticholinergic, which he isn't.
- **Allergies:** NKDA.
- **Family history:** No known family history of colorectal cancer or IBD, though admits he doesn't know his extended family's health history well.
- **Social history:** No smoking, moderate alcohol use, sedentary desk job.
- **ROS:** Denies fevers, denies weight loss, denies blood in stool, denies significant abdominal pain, denies urinary symptoms.

### Vitals (give on request)
BP 132/82, HR 74, RR 14, Temp 98.2°F, O2 sat 98% RA, BMI 27.

### Physical exam findings (reveal only for specific system/maneuver requested)
- **General:** Well-appearing, mildly uncomfortable discussing the topic but cooperative.
- **Abdominal exam:** Soft, mildly distended, non-tender, no palpable masses, no organomegaly, normal bowel sounds. Mild fullness palpable in the left lower quadrant consistent with retained stool (not a mass).
- **Digital rectal exam (if specifically requested -- appropriate to mention verbally rather than perform in this format):** Normal sphincter tone, no palpable mass, no gross blood on the glove -- a reasonable and relevant exam to at least mention performing or discuss given the constipation workup and his age.
- **Thyroid palpation:** Normal size, no nodules.

### Labs/imaging (give only if ordered)
- **TSH (if ordered):** Normal, reassuring against hypothyroidism as a contributor.
- **CBC (if ordered):** Normal, no anemia (an important reassuring negative -- iron-deficiency anemia would raise more concern for an occult GI source of blood loss).
- **BMP, including calcium (if ordered):** Normal (hypercalcemia is a less common but real secondary cause of constipation worth excluding).
- **Fecal occult blood test/FIT (if ordered):** Negative.
- **Colonoscopy (if referred):** Not performed at this visit -- correct plan is referral, and this visit is a good opportunity to get him started on age-appropriate colorectal cancer screening regardless of the constipation itself.

### Model answer for debrief
This is most consistent with functional constipation driven by low fiber intake, inadequate hydration, a sedentary lifestyle, and habitually delaying the urge to defecate -- a very common and correctable pattern. Colorectal malignancy is the most important diagnosis to actively screen for given his age and lack of prior screening, but is reasonably lower on the list here given the absence of blood in the stool, weight loss, or a change in stool caliber; regardless, this visit is an important opportunity to get him started on overdue age-appropriate colorectal cancer screening independent of the constipation itself. Hypothyroidism and medication-related causes are reasonable secondary causes to screen for and are not supported here. Plan: first-line lifestyle changes (fiber, hydration, exercise, responding to urges promptly), an osmotic or bulk-forming laxative if those aren't sufficient, baseline labs given this hasn't been worked up before, and a referral for colonoscopy given his age and screening gap -- a detail that's easy to miss if a candidate focuses only on the constipation itself.
