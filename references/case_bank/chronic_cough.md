# Case: Chronic Cough (Ambulatory)

**Setting:** Primary care / outpatient IM clinic
**Suggested time:** 10-12 minutes total

### Patient demographics
Name: Miriam Osei. Age 49. Female. Works as a schoolteacher. Non-smoker. Affect: mildly embarrassed/apologetic about "wasting time" on "just a cough."

### Primary diagnosis
Cough-variant presentation driven by a combination of post-nasal drip (upper airway cough syndrome) and mild GERD -- the two most common causes of chronic cough in a non-smoker with a normal chest X-ray.

### Key differentials
- **Lung malignancy [RED FLAG]** -- low pretest probability (non-smoker, no weight loss, no hemoptysis) but must be actively screened for given cough duration.
- Asthma / cough-variant asthma
- ACE-inhibitor-induced cough
- Post-nasal drip / upper airway cough syndrome (part of the actual picture here)
- GERD-related cough (part of the actual picture here)

### History key (reveal only as asked)
- **Duration:** 10 weeks, meets the definition of chronic cough (>8 weeks).
- **Character:** Dry, non-productive most of the time; occasionally brings up a small amount of clear mucus, especially in the morning.
- **Timing/triggers:** Worse at night and first thing in the morning. Also worse after lying down and after big meals. Notices frequent throat-clearing and a sensation of "stuff dripping down the back of my throat."
- **Associated symptoms:** Mild nasal congestion and post-nasal drip sensation most days. Occasional heartburn/regurgitation, especially after large or spicy meals and when lying down soon after eating -- has been self-treating with occasional antacids with some relief.
- **Red flag screening (if asked):**
  - **No hemoptysis.**
  - **No unintentional weight loss.**
  - No night sweats.
  - No fevers.
  - No chest pain.
  - No significant shortness of breath at rest (mild exertional breathlessness only when coughing hard, not otherwise).
- **Wheeze/asthma screen:** No known history of asthma or childhood wheezing. No clear seasonal or allergen-triggered pattern beyond general nasal congestion. No exercise-induced symptoms.
- **PMH:** Hypertension, well-controlled.
- **Medications:** Lisinopril 20mg daily (started about 4 months ago, before the cough started roughly 10 weeks ago -- timeline makes ACE-inhibitor cough plausible and worth considering, though the clear post-nasal drip and reflux symptoms are also present; a good answer should raise ACE-inhibitor cough as a contributor/differential even though it isn't the primary driver in this case). Occasional OTC antacid.
- **Allergies:** NKDA.
- **Family history:** Mother has "bad allergies" and chronic sinus problems. No family history of lung cancer or tuberculosis.
- **Social history:** Never smoker, no vaping, no occupational dust/chemical exposure (works in a classroom), no pets at home, no recent travel, no known TB exposure or contacts.
- **ROS:** Denies sore throat beyond throat-clearing, denies ear symptoms, denies joint pains, denies rashes.

### Vitals (give on request)
BP 128/80, HR 76, RR 16, Temp 98.6°F, O2 sat 99% RA, BMI 24.

### Physical exam findings (reveal only for specific system/maneuver requested)
- **General:** Well-appearing, no acute distress, occasional throat-clearing observed during the visit.
- **HEENT:** Mild cobblestoning of the posterior oropharynx, mild-to-moderate clear post-nasal drainage visible on oropharyngeal exam. Nasal mucosa mildly boggy/pale. No sinus tenderness to palpation.
- **Neck:** No lymphadenopathy, no thyromegaly.
- **Lung auscultation:** Clear bilaterally, no wheezes, no crackles, no rhonchi.
- **Cardiac auscultation:** Regular rate and rhythm, no murmurs.
- **Abdominal exam:** Soft, non-tender, no epigastric tenderness or masses.

### Labs/imaging (give only if ordered)
- **Chest X-ray (if ordered):** Normal, no mass, no infiltrate, no hilar lymphadenopathy.
- **Spirometry/PFTs (if ordered):** Normal spirometry, no obstructive pattern (argues against active asthma as the primary driver, though doesn't fully exclude cough-variant asthma).
- **CBC (if ordered):** Unremarkable, no eosinophilia.

### Model answer for debrief
The most likely picture is a combination of upper airway cough syndrome (post-nasal drip) and GERD, the two most common causes of chronic cough in a non-smoker with a normal exam, supported by nocturnal/post-meal worsening, throat-clearing, visible post-nasal drainage, and reflux symptoms. The ACE-inhibitor started 4 months ago is a real contributor worth addressing given the temporal association, even if it isn't the sole cause. Malignancy and cough-variant asthma are appropriately lower on the differential given the non-smoking history, lack of hemoptysis or weight loss, and clear exam, but should still be mentioned as considered. Reasonable plan: chest X-ray to complete the standard chronic cough workup, empiric trial of intranasal steroid for post-nasal drip, reflux precautions with consideration of a PPI trial, and switching the ACE-inhibitor to an alternative (e.g. an ARB) both diagnostically and therapeutically, with follow-up to reassess and escalate to spirometry or further imaging if the cough persists.
