# Case: Headache (Ambulatory)

**Setting:** Primary care / outpatient IM clinic
**Suggested time:** 10-12 minutes total

---
## FOR CLAUDE ONLY -- do not reveal this section directly; deliver only through the encounter flow
---

### Patient demographics
Name: Theresa Nakamura. Age 52. Female. Works as a librarian. Affect: worried -- a coworker's relative recently had a stroke and she's scared this might be "something serious," but also feels a little silly for "making a big deal" out of headaches.

### Primary diagnosis
Chronic tension-type headache, with a secondary component of medication-overuse headache from frequent OTC analgesic use.

### Key differentials
- **Giant cell (temporal) arteritis [RED FLAG]** -- age-appropriate (>50) and must be actively screened for (jaw claudication, scalp tenderness, visual changes, PMR symptoms) even though not present here.
- **Subarachnoid hemorrhage / other acute intracranial process [RED FLAG]** -- screened for via "thunderclap" onset question; this is a chronic, gradual pattern, not acute, so should be reassuring once asked about.
- Migraine (without aura) -- some overlapping features but doesn't fully fit the pattern here.
- Medication-overuse headache (part of the actual picture -- frequent OTC analgesic use is a real contributor).

### History key (reveal only as asked)
- **Onset:** Gradual, headaches have been present in some form for years, but have become more frequent over the last 3 months.
- **Location:** Bilateral, band-like, "like a tight cap" around the head, sometimes with some tightness in the neck/shoulders.
- **Character:** Dull, pressure-like, non-throbbing (argues against migraine).
- **Duration:** Hours at a time, sometimes most of the day.
- **Frequency:** Now occurring roughly 15+ days per month (meets frequency criteria worth flagging for chronic/medication-overuse pattern).
- **Aggravating:** Stress, long hours at a computer/reading, poor sleep.
- **Relieving:** OTC ibuprofen or acetaminophen, which she now takes almost daily -- "if I don't take something in the morning I know it'll turn into a bad headache."
- **Associated symptoms:** Denies nausea/vomiting, denies photophobia or phonophobia as prominent features (argues against migraine), denies visual aura or scintillating scotoma.
- **Red flag screening (if asked):**
  - **No thunderclap/"worst headache of my life" onset.**
  - **No jaw pain with chewing (jaw claudication).**
  - **No scalp tenderness (e.g. when brushing hair).**
  - **No new visual changes, vision loss, or double vision.**
  - No fever.
  - No neck stiffness.
  - No history of head trauma.
  - No neurologic symptoms: no weakness, no numbness, no speech changes, no confusion.
  - No worsening with position change, coughing, or straining (would suggest raised intracranial pressure).
  - No new headache pattern that is clearly different/worse than her usual (this is a gradual chronic pattern, not a new type of headache).
  - No proximal muscle pain/stiffness in shoulders and hips (polymyalgia rheumatica screen, associated with GCA).
- **PMH:** Mild hypertension, otherwise healthy.
- **Medications:** OTC ibuprofen or acetaminophen, taken 5-6 days/week for the last couple of months (key detail for medication-overuse headache). No prescription migraine or headache medications.
- **Allergies:** NKDA.
- **Family history:** Mother had migraines. No family history of stroke or aneurysm.
- **Social history:** No smoking, minimal alcohol, no drug use. High-stress period at work (library renovation, short-staffed). Sleep has been poor, 5-6 hours/night, screen time high.
- **ROS:** Denies weight loss, denies fevers/night sweats, denies visual symptoms, denies jaw pain, denies proximal joint/muscle stiffness.

### Vitals (give on request)
BP 138/86, HR 74, RR 14, Temp 98.2°F, O2 sat 99% RA.

### Physical exam findings (reveal only for specific system/maneuver requested)
- **General:** Mildly anxious-appearing, no acute distress.
- **HEENT:** No scalp tenderness to palpation, temporal arteries non-tender and normal pulsation bilaterally (important negative for GCA).
- **Neck:** Supple, no nuchal rigidity, mild tenderness/tightness of the trapezius and posterior neck muscles bilaterally (consistent with tension-type pattern).
- **Cranial nerves II-XII:** Grossly intact.
- **Fundoscopic exam (if performed):** No papilledema.
- **Neuro exam (motor/sensory/reflexes):** Normal throughout, no focal deficits.
- **Gait/coordination:** Normal.

### Labs/imaging (give only if ordered)
- **ESR/CRP (if ordered):** Within normal limits (helps further argue against GCA, though a good candidate should know these would still be worth checking given her age even with a reassuring exam).
- **Head CT/MRI (if ordered):** Not indicated given no red flags on history/exam; if ordered anyway -- normal, no acute findings.
- **CBC/BMP (if ordered):** Unremarkable.

### Differential Diagnosis (4 items)
- [ ] Tension-type headache as leading diagnosis

- [ ] Giant cell arteritis explicitly mentioned as a differential actively ruled out given her age
- [ ] Migraine mentioned and reasonably distinguished from the presentation

### Plan / Workup (5 items)
- [ ] Recommends tapering/limiting OTC analgesic use to address medication-overuse component
- [ ] Discusses non-pharmacologic management: stress reduction, sleep hygiene, ergonomics/screen breaks
- [ ] Considers a preventive medication if frequency remains high after addressing overuse
- [ ] Correctly does not order neuroimaging given the reassuring history/exam (or explains why imaging isn't needed if asked)
- [ ] Gives clear red-flag return precautions (sudden severe headache, vision changes, jaw pain, neurologic symptoms)

### Communication (3 items)
- [ ] Validates her concern about something serious rather than dismissing it outright
- [ ] Clearly explains the medication-overuse mechanism in understandable terms (this is often counterintuitive to patients)
- [ ] Clear closing summarizing the plan and safety-netting

### Model answer for debrief
This is most consistent with chronic tension-type headache complicated by medication-overuse headache, given the bilateral band-like non-throbbing pattern, absence of migrainous features, a clear stress/sleep trigger, and near-daily OTC analgesic use over the past couple of months. Given her age (>50) and new-onset-feeling worry, giant cell arteritis is the most important diagnosis to actively screen for and rule out -- here it's well argued against by the absence of jaw claudication, scalp tenderness, visual changes, or proximal muscle stiffness, plus a normal temporal artery exam. Subarachnoid hemorrhage and other acute intracranial causes are excluded by the clearly gradual, chronic pattern rather than a thunderclap or new headache type. Plan: address the medication-overuse component directly (this is often the single highest-yield intervention and is frequently missed), non-pharmacologic measures for the tension component (stress management, sleep, ergonomics), consider a preventive agent if frequency remains high after the taper, avoid unnecessary neuroimaging given the reassuring picture, and give clear red-flag precautions for the future.
