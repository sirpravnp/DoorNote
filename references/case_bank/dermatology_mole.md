**Setting:** Primary care / outpatient IM clinic

### Patient demographics
Name: Gary Holt. Age 61. Male. Retired landscaper (high lifetime sun exposure). Affect: casual, tends to minimize ("probably nothing, my wife made me come in").

### Primary diagnosis
Clinically suspicious pigmented lesion concerning for melanoma, warranting urgent dermatology referral for biopsy.

### Key differentials
- **Melanoma [RED FLAG]** -- the diagnosis this case is built around; must be recognized via ABCDE features.
- Atypical/dysplastic nevus (benign but a reasonable initial differential before biopsy)
- Seborrheic keratosis (benign mimic, worth mentioning as a differential a candidate might reasonably raise before examining it closely)

### History key (reveal only as asked)
- **Onset:** He first noticed it maybe 6 months ago, but thinks it's gotten bigger and darker over the last 2 months.
- **Location:** Upper back (an area he can't see well himself -- his wife spotted it).
- **Symptom changes:** No pain. Occasionally itches. No bleeding, though he thinks it "looked a little irritated" once after his shirt rubbed on it.
- **Prior similar lesions:** He has "a lot of moles," always has, never had one biopsied before.
- **Growth:** Confirms it does seem bigger than a few months ago; wife says the color looks "uneven, dark brown and almost black in spots."
- **Red flag screening (if asked):**

  - No unintentional weight loss.

- **Medications:** None regularly.
- **Allergies:** NKDA.
- **Family history:** No family history of melanoma or other skin cancer that he knows of.
- **Social history:** Worked outdoors as a landscaper for over 30 years, admits he rarely wore sunscreen or a hat. Several bad sunburns as a younger man. No smoking, social alcohol use.
- **ROS:** Otherwise unremarkable; denies fevers, denies other skin changes, denies fatigue.

### Physical exam findings (reveal only for specific system/maneuver requested)
- **General:** Fair-skinned, evidence of significant sun damage (multiple solar lentigines, actinic changes) on the face, forearms, and upper back.
- **Lesion inspection (the key exam -- reveal ABCDE features only as the user specifically inspects/describes what they're looking for):**
  - **Asymmetry:** Present -- one half doesn't mirror the other.
  - **Border:** Irregular, notched edges rather than smooth/round.
  - **Color:** Variegated -- shades of brown, black, and a small area that looks slightly pink/reddish.
  - **Diameter:** Approximately 8mm (>6mm, above the classic threshold of concern).
  - **Evolving:** Confirmed by history -- has changed in size and color over the past couple of months.
- **Surrounding skin:** No surrounding erythema, no satellite lesions visible.
- **Palpation:** Slightly raised/irregular surface, non-tender.
- **Regional lymph node exam (axillary, given the lesion is on the upper back):** No palpable lymphadenopathy.
- **Full skin exam (if performed):** A few other benign-appearing seborrheic keratoses and lentigines scattered on sun-exposed areas; no other lesions share the same concerning features.

### Model answer for debrief
This lesion has enough ABCDE features -- asymmetry, irregular borders, variegated color, diameter over 6mm, and a clear history of evolution -- to warrant prompt dermatology referral for biopsy; reassurance or routine follow-up would be a significant miss here. Benign mimics like a dysplastic nevus or seborrheic keratosis are reasonable to mention as differentials, but the combination of features (particularly the evolution and irregular border) should push a candidate toward urgency rather than watchful waiting. The correct next step is referral for excisional biopsy, not an in-office shave biopsy, since shave biopsy can compromise depth staging if this is melanoma. Sun-protection counseling and a full skin check are reasonable additions given his extensive sun-exposure history, but shouldn't substitute for the urgent referral.
