# Case: Palpitations and Weight Loss (Thyroid Disease, Ambulatory)

**Setting:** Primary care / outpatient IM clinic
**Suggested time:** 10-12 minutes total

---
## FOR CLAUDE ONLY -- do not reveal this section directly; deliver only through the encounter flow

### Patient demographics
Name: Priya Ramachandran. Age 34. Female. Works as a graphic designer. Affect: anxious, a little jittery in demeanor during the visit itself (consistent with the underlying diagnosis).

### Primary diagnosis
Hyperthyroidism, most consistent with Graves' disease.

### Key differentials
- Graves' disease (the diagnosis, and the most common cause of hyperthyroidism in a young woman)
- Toxic multinodular goiter / toxic adenoma (less likely at her age but worth mentioning as a differential; distinguished on exam by goiter character and would ultimately need labs/imaging to fully differentiate)
- Subacute (de Quervain's) thyroiditis -- worth considering but argued against by the absence of a preceding viral illness or thyroid pain/tenderness
- **Anxiety disorder alone [screen, not the answer here]** -- some overlapping symptoms, but the exam findings (goiter, eye findings, tremor, tachycardia, weight loss) point to an organic thyroid cause rather than a primary anxiety disorder; a good candidate should screen for and rule out an isolated psychiatric cause rather than assuming either way.

### History key (reveal only as asked)
- **Onset:** Gradual over the past 2-3 months.
- **Symptoms (if asked generally, "how have you been feeling"):** Feeling anxious and "on edge," trouble sleeping, heart racing at times.
- **Weight change:** Lost about 10 lbs over 2-3 months without trying, despite feeling like she's eating more than usual.
- **Heat/cold tolerance:** Heat intolerance -- finds herself needing the AC on when others are comfortable, sweating more than usual.
- **Cardiac symptoms:** Palpitations, sometimes feels her heart "racing" or "skipping," no chest pain, no syncope.
- **GI symptoms:** More frequent bowel movements than her baseline, though not true diarrhea.
- **Menstrual history:** Periods have become lighter and less regular over the past couple of months.
- **Eye symptoms (if asked):** Some mild eye irritation and a sense that her eyes "look different," friends have commented she looks like she's "staring."
- **Tremor:** Notices her hands shake sometimes, especially when holding something still like a cup of coffee.
- **Sleep:** Trouble falling asleep, feels wired at night.
- **Red flag screening (if asked):**
  - No chest pain.
  - No syncope or near-syncope.
  - No fever (argues somewhat against subacute thyroiditis, which often follows a viral illness with neck pain/tenderness).
  - No neck pain or tenderness that she's noticed.
  - No recent viral illness preceding these symptoms.
- **PMH:** No prior thyroid history. No prior psychiatric diagnosis, though she wonders herself if this is "just anxiety."
- **Medications:** Oral contraceptive pill, no other regular medications, no recent new supplements or stimulant use.
- **Allergies:** NKDA.
- **Family history:** Mother has an autoimmune condition (thinks it might be lupus or something similar, isn't fully sure), no known family history of thyroid disease specifically, though this is somewhat consistent with an autoimmune predisposition.
- **Social history:** No smoking (relevant -- smoking can worsen Graves' ophthalmopathy specifically), moderate caffeine intake which she's cut back on recently because it makes the palpitations worse, no recreational drug use.
- **ROS:** Denies chest pain, denies vision loss (though endorses the eye appearance change above), denies fevers, denies neck pain.

### Vitals (give on request)
BP 138/68 (widened pulse pressure, consistent with hyperthyroidism), HR 112 (tachycardic), RR 18, Temp 99.1°F (mildly elevated), O2 sat 99% RA, weight down from her documented weight 6 months ago consistent with her reported loss.

### Physical exam findings (reveal only for specific system/maneuver requested)
- **General:** Appears anxious, fidgety, mild tremor visible at rest.
- **Eyes:** Mild proptosis (bulging appearance) bilaterally, mild lid retraction with visible sclera above the iris, no significant periorbital edema, extraocular movements intact (no diplopia).
- **Neck/thyroid palpation:** Diffusely enlarged, smooth, non-tender thyroid gland (goiter); if auscultated, a soft bruit may be appreciated over the gland, consistent with the increased vascularity of Graves' disease.
- **Cardiac auscultation:** Tachycardic, regular rhythm, no murmurs.
- **Skin:** Warm, moist skin; fine tremor noted when hands are outstretched.
- **Reflexes:** Brisk (hyperreflexic), a notable contrast to the delayed relaxation phase seen in hypothyroidism.
- **Lymph node exam:** No lymphadenopathy.

### Labs/imaging (give only if ordered)
- **TSH:** Suppressed/low.
- **Free T4:** Elevated.
- **Free T3 (if ordered):** Elevated.
- **TSH receptor antibodies (TRAb) or thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulins (TSI), if ordered:** Positive, supporting Graves' disease specifically as the cause.
- **Radioactive iodine uptake scan (if ordered/discussed):** Would show diffusely increased uptake in Graves' disease (as opposed to focal uptake in a toxic nodule, or low/absent uptake in thyroiditis) -- a good candidate can mention this as a way to distinguish the causes of hyperthyroidism even if it isn't ordered at this visit.
- **ECG (if ordered, given tachycardia):** Sinus tachycardia, no arrhythmia.

### Model answer for debrief
This presentation -- weight loss despite increased appetite, heat intolerance, palpitations, a diffusely enlarged non-tender goiter with a bruit, lid retraction and mild proptosis, tremor, and hyperreflexia -- is most consistent with Graves' disease, the most common cause of hyperthyroidism in a young woman. Toxic multinodular goiter and subacute thyroiditis are reasonable differentials but are argued against by her age/diffuse (rather than nodular) goiter and the absence of a preceding viral illness or thyroid tenderness, respectively. A primary anxiety disorder should be explicitly considered and screened for given the overlapping symptoms, but the constellation of objective findings here (goiter, ophthalmopathy, hyperreflexia, tachycardia with a widened pulse pressure) points to an organic thyroid cause rather than anxiety alone. Plan: confirm with TSH/free T4 (suppressed TSH, elevated free T4), consider TRAb/TSI or an uptake scan to confirm Graves' specifically, start a beta-blocker for symptomatic relief while workup proceeds, refer to endocrinology to discuss definitive treatment options, and counsel on smoking avoidance given her eye findings.
