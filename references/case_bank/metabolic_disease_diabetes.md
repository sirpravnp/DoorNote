# Case: New Diabetes Diagnosis (Metabolic Disease, Ambulatory)

**Setting:** Primary care / outpatient IM clinic -- follow-up visit for abnormal labs from a recent physical
**Suggested time:** 10-12 minutes total

---
## FOR CLAUDE ONLY -- do not reveal this section directly; deliver only through the encounter flow
---

### Patient demographics
Name: Marcus Bellamy. Age 46. Male. Works as a truck driver (long sedentary hours, irregular meal schedule). Affect: a bit overwhelmed/defensive about his weight and diet, worried about what this means for his life ("my dad lost his leg to diabetes").

### Primary diagnosis
New type 2 diabetes mellitus, in the context of obesity and dyslipidemia (metabolic syndrome picture).

### Key differentials
- Type 2 diabetes mellitus (the diagnosis)
- Type 1 diabetes / LADA (worth a brief mental differential given adult-onset, but clinical picture -- gradual onset, obesity, no ketosis symptoms -- strongly favors type 2)
- Secondary causes of hyperglycemia (steroid use, pancreatic disease) -- worth screening for and reasonably excluded by history

### History key (reveal only as asked)
- **How this came up:** Routine annual physical bloodwork 2 weeks ago showed an elevated fasting glucose and A1c; he's here for the follow-up discussion.
- **Symptoms (if asked about classic hyperglycemia symptoms):** On reflection, admits to increased thirst and more frequent urination over the past couple of months, which he'd chalked up to drinking more coffee on long drives. Mild fatigue. No unintentional weight loss (has actually gained weight). No blurry vision. No recurrent infections that he's noticed, though he mentions a slow-healing cut on his foot from a few weeks ago.
- **Red flag screening (if asked):**
  - No nausea, vomiting, or abdominal pain (DKA screen -- reassuring, consistent with type 2 rather than a ketosis-prone presentation).
  - No confusion or altered mental status.
  - No rapid unintentional weight loss (argues against type 1/LADA or an occult malignancy causing secondary hyperglycemia).
  - No chest pain, no vision changes.
- **Diet/lifestyle:** Diet is heavy on fast food and gas-station snacks given his driving schedule, drinks regular soda most days, minimal fruits/vegetables. Very little structured exercise -- sits most of the day for work. Sleep is irregular due to shift patterns, often 5-6 hours.
- **Weight history:** Has gradually gained about 30 lbs over the past 5 years. Has tried a few diets in the past without lasting success.
- **PMH:** Hypertension diagnosed 2 years ago, on medication. Dyslipidemia noted on the same recent labs (not previously treated). No prior diabetes diagnosis or prediabetes documented, though admits he hasn't seen a doctor regularly.
- **Medications:** Amlodipine for blood pressure. No diabetes medications yet (this is the visit to start that conversation). No steroid use.
- **Allergies:** NKDA.
- **Family history:** Father has type 2 diabetes with complications, including a partial foot amputation. Mother has hypertension.
- **Social history:** No smoking. Drinks a few beers on weekends. No recreational drug use. Married, two kids; stressed about balancing work schedule with healthy habits.
- **ROS:** Denies chest pain, denies vision changes, denies numbness/tingling in his feet currently (though endorses the slow-healing foot cut above), denies erectile dysfunction if specifically asked (a reasonable question in a new diabetes visit, though sensitive -- fine either way if he's asked or not).

### Vitals (give on request)
BP 142/88, HR 78, RR 14, Temp 98.4°F, O2 sat 98% RA, BMI 33 (obese), waist circumference elevated (if specifically measured/asked).

### Physical exam findings (reveal only for specific system/maneuver requested)
- **General:** Overweight/obese habitus, otherwise well-appearing.
- **Skin/acanthosis nigricans check (if specifically examined -- neck/axillae):** Mild acanthosis nigricans present at the posterior neck, consistent with insulin resistance.
- **Cardiac auscultation:** Regular rate and rhythm, no murmurs.
- **Lung auscultation:** Clear bilaterally.
- **Abdominal exam:** Obese, soft, non-tender, no organomegaly appreciated.
- **Monofilament foot exam (if performed -- this is the key targeted exam for this case):** Sensation intact bilaterally to monofilament testing at this visit -- no peripheral neuropathy yet detected, though this should be repeated annually given the new diagnosis.
- **Foot inspection:** The slow-healing cut mentioned in history is visible on the plantar surface of the right foot, small, healing but not fully closed, no surrounding erythema or signs of infection currently.
- **Pulses (dorsalis pedis/posterior tibial, if checked):** 2+ and symmetric.

### Labs/imaging (give only if ordered, or reference the labs that prompted this visit if asked)
- **Fasting glucose (from the visit 2 weeks ago):** 162 mg/dL (elevated; diabetes range).
- **A1c (from the same visit):** 8.1% (elevated, consistent with diabetes).
- **Lipid panel:** LDL 168, HDL 36, Triglycerides 240, Total cholesterol 250 (significant dyslipidemia, not yet treated).
- **BMP (if ordered):** Normal renal function -- relevant for metformin dosing decisions.
- **Urine microalbumin (if ordered):** Not yet checked -- correct step is to order this as part of the new-diagnosis workup.
- **Repeat/confirmatory testing:** If asked how the diagnosis is confirmed, two abnormal tests (the fasting glucose and A1c both being in the diabetic range) on the same draw is sufficient to confirm the diagnosis without needing to repeat.

- [ ] Asks about classic hyperglycemia symptoms: polyuria, polydipsia, fatigue, blurry vision
- [ ] Asks about unintentional weight loss (helps distinguish type 2 from type 1/LADA or secondary causes)
- [ ] **[RED FLAG]** Screens for DKA symptoms: nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion
- [ ] Asks about slow-healing wounds/infections
- [ ] Asks about numbness/tingling in the feet (neuropathy screen)
- [ ] Takes a diet and physical activity history
- [ ] Asks about family history of diabetes and its complications
- [ ] Elicits sleep pattern/shift-work impact on lifestyle
- [ ] **[RED FLAG]** Screens for secondary causes: steroid use, other endocrine symptoms
- [ ] Asks about prior diabetes/prediabetes screening history
- [ ] Explores his emotional response/concerns (his father's amputation) rather than just the medical facts
- [ ] Asks about erectile dysfunction or other complication-related symptoms (optional but appropriate)

### Physical Exam (5 items)
- [ ] Measures/notes BMI and waist circumference
- [ ] Checks for acanthosis nigricans
- [ ] Performs monofilament foot exam
- [ ] Inspects the feet for wounds/skin breakdown
- [ ] Checks peripheral pulses

### Differential Diagnosis (3 items)
- [ ] Type 2 diabetes named as the diagnosis, with reasoning (gradual onset, obesity, no ketosis features)
- [ ] Briefly considers and reasonably excludes type 1/LADA
- [ ] Identifies the co-existing dyslipidemia and hypertension as part of a broader metabolic syndrome picture

### Plan / Workup (7 items)
- [ ] Discusses starting metformin as first-line pharmacotherapy
- [ ] Discusses lifestyle modification: diet, physical activity, weight loss goals
- [ ] Orders/discusses urine microalbumin (diabetic nephropathy screening)
- [ ] Recommends ophthalmology referral for a diabetic eye exam
- [ ] Addresses the dyslipidemia (statin therapy)
- [ ] Discusses A1c goal and follow-up interval for repeat testing
- [ ] Addresses the non-healing foot wound directly and reinforces foot care/self-monitoring

### Communication (3 items)
- [ ] Acknowledges his fear about his father's outcome without dismissing or over-alarming him
- [ ] Explains the diagnosis and plan in plain, non-judgmental language (avoids shaming him about diet/weight)
- [ ] Clear closing summarizing the plan and follow-up timeline

### Model answer for debrief
This is new type 2 diabetes mellitus, confirmed by a fasting glucose of 162 and A1c of 8.1% on the same draw, in a patient with obesity, physical exam evidence of insulin resistance (acanthosis nigricans), and a strong family history. Type 1/LADA is reasonably excluded by the gradual onset, absence of ketosis symptoms, and weight gain rather than loss. Co-existing dyslipidemia and hypertension complete a metabolic syndrome picture that should be addressed together, not in isolation. Plan: start metformin, initiate a statin for the dyslipidemia, set concrete lifestyle goals around diet and activity given his driving schedule, order a urine microalbumin and refer for a diabetic eye exam as baseline complication screening, and address the non-healing foot wound directly given his father's history of amputation -- both as a practical wound-care issue and as an opportunity to reinforce foot self-care. A same- or next-visit conversation acknowledging his fear, rather than only reciting the medical plan, is an important part of a strong answer here.
